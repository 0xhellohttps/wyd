NEW YORK PILOT · PRE-LAUNCH · AUG 2026

The whole argument,
written out.

The deck is fourteen slides and takes about four minutes. This is the same case at full length, for anyone who wants the reasoning rather than the summary — what it is, who wants it, how it makes money, what the plan is, and what we can honestly claim today.

01 — THE PROBLEM

Nobody owns the decision. Everybody owns a slice of the aftermath.

Payments see the transaction. Social sees the engagement. Ticketing sees attendance. The reservation book sees one hour of a five-hour night. Not one of them holds the causal sequence that connects them — and the person who actually caused it gets nothing at all.

Planning is the tax

Four apps, eleven tabs, one person stuck organising, and a group chat that dies on Thursday. Repeat two or three times a week.

Recommendation dead-ends

A video makes you want a place and hands you nothing — no table, no time, no group, no price. Every food post is a night that was never executable.

Taste has no record

The friend who put you onto the room gets one sentence at a table. The venue gets the covers, the platform gets the data, they get nothing.

02 — THE PRODUCT

Three texts and the night is booked.

“friday? me + 3. want to see ANOTR” → it comes back with a sequenced night at a real number per person → “book it”. Table held, tickets in every wallet, cars at both ends, everyone charged. No app to download, no account to make.

Three texts

Say it, see the night, say yes. Dinner, tickets, cars and the split come back booked.

The Playlist

A night becomes an object other people can run — not a review, the evening itself.

Remix

Change someone's night and inherit their audience. Distribution without having built one.

Date night

Staged, with a private ask before each stage. Nobody has to be the one who ends it.

Night in

Delivery, dinner and what you're watching — timed and split like any other night.

Group nights

A threshold. Stays a draft until eight are in, then books all at once. Price drops per head.

Conditions

No ceiling, or only if she's in, or anywhere but where he'll be. Constraints that aren't price.

Markets

Take a position on who's actually better. Settles on behaviour, not votes.

Rewards

Every month the agent comps a night to whoever actually moved people. Behaviour earns it, not follower count.

03 — THE INSIGHT

Everyone has that one friend who always knows a place.

WHAT WE'RE BUILDING, PUT PLAINLY

That person is doing unpaid A&R for an entire city. Music made that a real job with a real title — find it early, back it, get credited when it works. Restaurants and live experiences have exactly the same function running inside every friend group, and none of the infrastructure.

Provenance makes it visible. Standing makes it comparable. Markets make it worth something. That is the company.

04 — THE MARKET

The generation that treats going out as the default.

Frequent, not special

Going out isn't the occasion for people under forty — it's the week. The planning cost gets paid two or three times every week, which is why people default to the same four places.

Social before culinary

Nobody picks on food alone. They pick on who's coming, how loud it is, whether you can stay and what's walkable after. A rating of the cooking answers a question nobody asked.

Spent, not saved

Going out is one of the largest discretionary lines this group has, and the number is set by a plan nobody wrote down — decided in fragments across four apps.

05 — HOW IT MAKES MONEY

Four lines, one engine.

Every one of them settles on the same unit — a booked night with names and a provenance chain attached — which is why they compound rather than compete.

01
Take rate on the nightCONSUMER

A cut of what gets booked — tickets, cover, bundles, delivery. The unit is a booked night, and it scales with the thing we already do.

02
Brand-authored nightsBRAND

A brand publishes a night and pays for booked covers with provenance attached, not impressions. Competes for committed influencer and experiential budget.

03
Operator toolsSUPPLY

Venues connect ticketing, POS and staffing, read demand against the same graph the consumer agent plans against, and release inventory into it.

04
Market rakeNETWORK

A small take on positions in the taste markets — which also produces the ranking signal everything else depends on.

06 — WHY EVERYONE SAYS YES

The rare case where nobody has to lose.

Most marketplaces start by taking margin off one side to bribe the other. This one doesn't, because the thing being created — a night that actually happens, with the chain that caused it attached — didn't exist before and is worth something different to each party.

THE HARDEST ONE, FIRST Why a restaurant actually wants this

Restaurants are pitched software constantly and buy almost none of it. This isn't software. It's a party of four with a card already attached, arriving on a night the restaurant chose to want them.

Aimable demand

The problem was never Friday at 8. It's Tuesday, the 5:30 seating, and the back half of the room. Because the agent plans the whole evening, it can be pointed at the shifts that need filling rather than the ones already full.

No-shows stop existing

The split is charged when the night is confirmed, not when everyone shows up. A table that's already paid for is a fundamentally different asset from a reservation someone might abandon at 7:55.

Bigger parties, planned earlier

Groups are worth more per cover and are the hardest to coordinate, which is exactly the coordination the agent removes. Fours and eights instead of the two-top that books at the last minute.

Attribution they've never had

For the first time an operator can see which guests arrived because of whose taste. Not a channel report — the actual person, and the chain from them to the booking.

Guests arrive right

They know the time, the room, what it costs and what's after. Tables turn on schedule because the evening was sequenced around the kitchen, not in spite of it.

Nothing gets ripped out

They keep their book, their POS, their ticketing. We arrive as demand on top of what they already run — which is the only integration an operator will actually say yes to.

Diners

The planning tax disappears. One number for the whole night, split before anyone leaves, nothing booked without a tap, and the group chat stops needing a volunteer to run it.

Venues

Release inventory against a live forecast, pair the right artist with the right room, and see which pairings actually produce good nights instead of guessing at it a season ahead.

Creators

Provable attribution instead of a flat fee and a discount code, standing that can't be bought with follower count, and a comped night every month they move real behaviour.

Brands

Booked covers with a chain attached instead of impressions against an unverified audience — from budget that is already committed to this exact cohort.

07 — DISTRIBUTION

Three surfaces hold the pieces. None hold the night.

HAS THE INTENT Raya

Creates the highest-stakes planning problem there is, then stops at “so where should we go?” Date night finishes the sentence.

HAS THE INVENTORY Resy

Knows a table was wanted. Doesn't know the ticket before, the room after, or the four people splitting it.

HAS THE TASTE TikTok

Where discovery happens and where it terminates. Every food video is a Playlist nobody made executable.

WHO UNDERWRITES IT CPG budgets already paying these creators

Beauty, beverage and snack brands spend against this exact cohort through these exact creators, and get reach back. The same money against an authored night buys booked covers with attribution. No new budget line required.

08 — GO TO MARKET

Seed it with the people who already do this for free.

The cold-start problem and the distribution problem are the same problem, and one group of people solves both. NYC food creators are already authoring nights — badly, in a format that can't be booked and can't be attributed. Give twenty of them a night that is bookable and the loop starts turning on its own.

Why the creator says yes

Right now nobody can prove a post caused a booking, so they get paid a flat fee and a discount code. Provenance proves it, permanently, on the object. That is a materially better deal than what they are offered today.

Why the restaurant says yes

Attributed covers instead of unattributable walk-ins, with no rip-and-replace of the book or the POS. We arrive as a party of four with a card attached — the one thing every operator wants more of.

Why the brand says yes

CPG budgets already fund these exact creators for reach. The same spend against an authored night reports in booked covers with a chain attached, which is the only version that survives a marketing review asking for incrementality.

Target mid-tier, not mega

A four-million-follower food account is reach without concentration. The right first cohort is the opinionated, neighbourhood-anchored NYC reviewer — The VIP List, Brian Lindo, Jeremy Jacobowitz, @carboholic, @janemotion. Opinionated means falsifiable, which is what markets and standing need. Verify resident-audience share, never follower count.

Comped nights are the retention loop

Every month the top authors by replays, first-to-room and nights actually run get their next night booked and paid. It costs a venue less than the covers that author sent it, it can't be bought with follower count, and the reward is itself a night — published, replayed and traced. The incentive never leaves the system.

Twenty authors, one city, one season

Density beats coverage. Twenty creators publishing weekly in one city produce enough overlapping nights that the graph starts predicting rather than guessing — and enough attributed covers to walk into restaurant conversations with numbers instead of a deck.

09 — WHY NOW

The rails arrived and nobody has pointed them at this.

VERIFIED Rails arrived

Agent payment tooling shipped in the last 18 months — Stripe's Agentic Commerce Suite and Issuing for agents, Mastercard Agent Pay, Visa Intelligent Commerce.

VERIFIED But nobody's used them here

Every documented live example is retail goods. No agentic rail has a shipped restaurant, ticketing or transport case. The vertical is open.

OBSERVED Discovery broke

TikTok became where recommendation happens, and it dead-ends completely — a video makes you want a place and hands you nothing.

VERIFIED Supply is consolidating

Amex owns Resy and Tock and is partnered with Toast. The reservation layer is closing, which makes the layer above it the one worth owning.

10 — THE PLAN

Built as though the API never arrives.

Every comparable company — Resy, Tock, SevenRooms — started with direct, manual, single-city relationships and reached network partnership years later, unlocked by capital rather than a developer application. We plan for that shape rather than hoping against it.

0–6 MONTHS
One city, direct restaurants, concierge fulfilment

Hand-picked New York list. Relationships signed room by room, exactly how Resy and Tock both started. Deep links for cars and tickets. No platform integration claimed, because none exists.

6–18 MONTHS
Certified on Toast, Stripe carries the money

Toast Orders API through certification gets the outcome half of the loop. Stripe Issuing for agents as the instrument. Human approval stays in the flow — nobody has shipped unattended spend.

18–36 MONTHS
Reservation access, or the acquisition conversation

Earliest plausible window for a Resy or OpenTable calibre deal. Precedent says 2.5–6.5 years and it is always unlocked by capital or acquisition, never a developer application.

11 — WHERE WE ARE

Honestly stated.

What exists

The product design, the object model and the network mechanics are fully specified — see the product and the thesis. The Toast and Stripe integration path is researched against primary sources, in the strategy doc.

What doesn't yet

No platform integration, no signed restaurant partners, no users. Every number and name on these pages is illustrative. We are pre-launch and say so on every surface — the alternative is claims that don't survive diligence.

What we need

Capital to run a dense New York pilot with concierge fulfilment, a founding restaurant cohort, and a first creator group who want provable attribution more than a flat fee.

What proves it

One number: whether a published night gets replayed by someone who wasn't there — and whether the second person's night is as good as the first. Everything else follows from that.
