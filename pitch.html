01 — THE PROBLEM

Nobody owns the decision.
Everybody owns a slice of the aftermath.

Payments see the transaction. Social sees the engagement. Ticketing sees attendance. The reservation book sees one hour of a five-hour night.

Planning is the tax

Four apps, eleven tabs, one person stuck organising, a group chat that dies on Thursday. Two or three times a week.

Recommendation dead-ends

A video makes you want a place and hands you nothing. No table, no time, no group, no price.

Taste has no record

The friend who put you onto the room gets one sentence at a table. The venue gets the covers.

Everyone has that one friend
who always knows a place.

07 — WHY RESTAURANTS WANT IT

Not software. A party of four with a card attached.

Aimable demand

The problem was never Friday at 8. It's Tuesday, the 5:30 seating and the back half of the room.

No-shows stop existing

The split is charged at confirmation. A paid table is a different asset from a reservation.

Bigger parties, planned earlier

Groups are worth more per cover and are the hardest to coordinate. That's the coordination we remove.

Attribution they've never had

Which guests arrived because of whose taste — the person, and the chain to the booking.

Guests arrive right

Sequenced around the kitchen, so tables turn on schedule.

Nothing gets ripped out

They keep the book, the POS, the ticketing. We arrive as demand on top.

Resy — has the inventory

10 — WHY NOW

The rails arrived. Nobody has pointed them here.

Rails shipped

Stripe's Agentic Commerce Suite and Issuing for agents, Mastercard Agent Pay, Visa Intelligent Commerce — all inside 18 months.

But not in this vertical

Every documented live example is retail goods. No agentic rail has a shipped restaurant, ticketing or transport case.

Discovery broke

TikTok became where recommendation happens, and it dead-ends completely.

Supply is consolidating

Amex owns Resy and Tock and is partnered with Toast. The layer below is closing, so the layer above is the one to own.
